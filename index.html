Diabetes-related kidney disease may develop silently before noticeable symptoms appear.

For people with type 2 diabetes, kidney health should be assessed at diagnosis and at least annually using both UACR and eGFR, as clinically appropriate.

Visual Comparison: Kidney Health

Healthy Kidney

  • Intact glomerular filtration barrier
  • Retains essential albumin in blood
  • Normal UACR & stable eGFR

Diabetic Kidney Disease

  • Glomerular stress allows albumin leakage
  • Often silent with zero early symptoms
  • Confirmed via repeat UACR & eGFR evaluation
Key Clinical Learning Points
  • Combine UACR (albuminuria) and eGFR (filtration) for comprehensive diabetic kidney screening.
  • Absence of symptoms does not rule out early kidney injury—proactive testing is essential.
  • A single elevated UACR requires confirmation (commonly 2 of 3 samples over 3–6 months) within clinical context.
